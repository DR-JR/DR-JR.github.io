Ryne McCormick While most of the links in the activities section of the Lesson 1 wiki article were broken or dead web pages, some of the ones that did work contained some great information. While I would consider myself an advanced user of computers, I am always open to learning new things. One of these is how to use the command Traceroute. I never knew there was a powershell command to backtrack your connection to a website and see the progress and hops along the way. This will be very useful in diagnosing internet issues in the future, and connectivity problems between both internal and external networks. It was cool to find a use for a computer's Ping that isn't just an indication of how stable your connection is to counter-strike. I am familiar with the concepts presented in the rest of the activities presented in the wiki, however it's never a bad idea to brush up on concepts. Saving files, creating new filing systems, keyboard and mouse shortcuts, downloading and uploading files are all things I've been overly familiar with my entire life and it's interesting to see how these skills are presented to someone who may be experiencing a computer for the very first time. The tutorials were very straight-forward and easy to understand and follow and I am sure they are helping the people who need them. Another thing I was unfamiliar with though, is Git. I've used github briefly in the past for downloading scripts and other random files, but never really bothered to investigate it further than the surface visits. I've now come to understand it as a highly valuable collaborative tool that a large group of people can use to simultaneously work on different aspects of a larger project. Branching seems particularly useful when experimenting or looking for a different solution or outcome. I think this will be very useful for any group projects in classes, and eventually team projects in a collaborative work setting. Gaining a fundamental understanding of how to best use Git to my advantage now can only pay off in the future!
