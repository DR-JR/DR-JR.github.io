Ryne McCormick Tutorials Point Web Browser- The tutorial on web browsers was actually technically informative to me as someone who has used browsers since Windows 95 I have a well-defined set of skills I can execute in a browser environment, however I don't really understand all of the under-the-hood details still. Install multiple browsers to compare- I use Firefox and Chrome all day long between work and school and have been using them regularly for nearly 40 years combined. I have previously used Internet Explorer and don't care for it, in an era when Firefox and Chrome were leading the way in web browsers IE fell behind in terms of customization and add-ons and security, and by the time it caught up to its competitors it felt like pairing it with every Windows install was a desperate attempt to keep it relevant. In this instance I installed Brave, having never heard of it before. I understand it to be a privacy browser as it blocks trackers and ads natively without needing to search for add-ons. If you do turn on ads you can enable a feature where the browser rewards your ad viewing with crypto tokens that can be exchanged for further digital services later, which seems like a good way to trick people into watching ads again. I think I will remain with Firefox and my add-ons that block all of the unwelcome and intrusive advertisements. Configure browser extensions and add-ons- Brave claims to have a whole new way of indexing the internet and offers a brand new search engine. I found it to be reliably fast and also as a bonus was not full of garbage ads and promoted links and sponsored content at the top of the search results. It did however have an automatic summary created by some sort of chatbot, which muddies the browser a bit to me. I am fully capable of doing my own digging through articles and journals online if I need more information. I am from the times of Ask Jeeves and Lycos before the word 'Google' was synonymous with 'to look up'. Going to install extensions was easy as it relies on the Chrome store as it is built on Chromium. The extensions were easy to download and install. I try not to overload my browsers with too many extensions but I have my ad blockers and privacy extensions and they seem to work fine. Compare browser HTML5 compatibility- It seems the reasons internet explorer is the butt of browser jokes may have something to do with its HTML5 compatibility, as nearly every category is a MAYBE for supported functionality, while other top browsers like Firefox, Chrome, and Edge all have full or nearly full support.
